Methodology

How this board is built

What it is

Crisis Watch Africa is an automated conflict early-warning board. A collector visits a set of public RSS and Atom feeds on a schedule, keeps the reporting that is security-related and African, files it under terrorism, armed conflict or kidnapping & abduction, tags country and region, extracts reported casualties and scores severity, then writes a static data file that the site renders.

Collection pipeline

  • Fetch — pull 40+ public feeds concurrently: pan-African outlets, national media, think tanks and specialist conflict desks.
  • Clean — strip markup and illegal XML characters, parse publication times into UTC, discard anything older than 90 days.
  • De-duplicate — normalise the headline into a fingerprint so the same story syndicated by several outlets is stored once.
  • Geo-tag — match a country lexicon longest-name-first (so "Niger" never wins over "Nigeria"), then fall back to regional terms and the feed's default country.
  • Classify — weighted keyword scoring with the priority kidnapping > terrorism > conflict. Stories that carry security wording but none of the three categories land in "Other security news".
  • Full text — feeds that publish the whole article inside content:encoded are stored as-is; for the rest the collector visits the article page and pulls the paragraphs out. Results are cached in data/bodies.json so a story is never fetched twice.
  • Extract casualties — regex patterns for phrasings such as "at least 12 killed" or "death toll rises to 30", read from the headline and the opening of the article; only figures actually printed are used.

Regional split

Follows the convention used by most conflict-research organisations: the Sahel (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad, Mauritania), West Africa, the Horn of Africa (including Sudan, South Sudan, Somalia, Ethiopia, Kenya and Uganda), Central Africa (including DR Congo, the Central African Republic and Cameroon), North Africa and Southern Africa.

Known limitations

  • Coverage follows the openness of the sources. Remote conflict zones are under-reported, so a low count means low coverage, not low risk.
  • Keyword classification misfires, especially when one incident is both a terrorist attack and an abduction.
  • Casualty figures come from media reporting; the parties disagree and numbers are revised as events unfold.
  • Headlines and summaries are reproduced from the source (mostly in English, sometimes French or Spanish). Nothing here is translated or independently verified.
  • A minority of records only carry a short teaser, because the publisher blocks automated readers or exposes nothing but an excerpt in its feed. Those are marked "not available" on the record page and link straight to the original.

Refreshing the data

Run the collector from the project root; the pages do not need rebuilding:

python scraper/scrape.py

It writes data/news.json and data/news.js, and appends the daily tally to data/history.json.
